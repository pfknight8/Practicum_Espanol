# Pretérito

The Preterite tense serves as the "simple past" in Spanish. It is anything but simple, though, as you will see. Like the present, there is a regular pattern for conjugating these verbs based on their endings, but there are plenty of irregular conjugations to contend with here, as well.

## Conjugations

Like with the present tense, regular conjugations will follow the pattern based on the verb's ending--'-ar,' '-er,' or '-ir'--and will involve maintaining the 'root' or 'stem' of the verb whilest changing the ending based on the voice and number being referred to. These are provided in the tables that follow.

### *-ar verbs:*

| Singular       | Ending | Plural           | Ending   |
|:---------------|-------:|:-----------------|---------:|
| Yo             | -é     | Nosotros         | -amos    |
| Tu             | -aste  | Vosotros         | -asteis  |
| Él/Ella/Usted  | -ó     | Ellos/as/Ustedes | -aron    |

### *-er & -ir verbs:*

| Singular       | Ending | Plural           | Ending   |
|:---------------|-------:|:-----------------|---------:|
| Yo             | -í     | Nosotros         | -imos    |
| Tu             | -iste  | Vosotros         | -isteis  |
| Él/Ella/Usted  | -ió    | Ellos/as/Ustedes | -ieron   |

### *Irregular verbs*

There are many verbs that break these patterns, unfortunately, and this involves modifications to the verb stem, as well as changes to the ending patterns (largely in removing the '´' accent mark). Fortunately, there are some regular patterns to these, forming so-called "regular irregulars." In some cases, though, they are unique and simply have to be memorized.

These are -ir verbs that experience a vowel shift (both e -> i and o -> u) in their stem, but unlike in the above group(s), here this occurrs only in both of the 3rd person forms.

| Verb       | Yo         | Tu         | Él/Ella/Usted | Nosotros   | Vosotros   | Ellos/as/Ustedes |
