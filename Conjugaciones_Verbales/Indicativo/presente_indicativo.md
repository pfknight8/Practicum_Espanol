# The Present Indicative

This verb tense is the most general, and is usually the first to be taught (in any language, really).

## Conjugations

Regular conjugations will follow the pattern based on the verb's ending--'-ar,' '-er,' or '-ir'--and will involve maintaining the 'root' or 'stem' of the verb whilest changing the ending based on the voice and number being referred to. These are provided in the tables that follow.

### *-ar verbs:*

| Singular       | Ending | Plural           | Ending   |
|:---------------|-------:|:-----------------|---------:|
| Yo             | -o     | Nosotros         | -amos    |
| Tu             | -as    | Vosotros         | -áis     |
| Él/Ella/Usted  | -a     | Ellos/as/Ustedes | -an      |

### *-er verbs:*

| Singular       | Ending | Plural           | Ending   |
|:---------------|-------:|:-----------------|---------:|
| Yo             | -o     | Nosotros         | -emos    |
| Tu             | -es    | Vosotros         | -éis     |
| Él/Ella/Usted  | -e     | Ellos/as/Ustedes | -en      |

### *-ir verbs:*

| Singular       | Ending | Plural           | Ending   |
|:---------------|-------:|:-----------------|---------:|
| Yo             | -o     | Nosotros         | -imos    |
| Tu             | -es    | Vosotros         | -ís      |
| Él/Ella/Usted  | -e     | Ellos/as/Ustedes | -en      |

### *Irregular verbs*

There are many irregular verbs, some of which fall into the sort of "regular irregular" patterns. Many of these are simple stem-changing verbs, where there is a vowel change in the stem--this change is typically not performed in the 'nosotros' and 'vostotros' forms. There are also those that change the "Yo"-form only. Others are what we will call the "usual suspects" of "fully irregular" verbs, which must be learned by experience.
