# Presente Perfecto

The Present Perfect tense, also called the "pretérito perfecto compuesto" in spanish, is a compound, "perfect" tense, and is formed similar to other perfect tenses by using a form of 'haber' as an auxiliary verb and the participle form of a verb.

This tense is used for actions that occurred in the past and are ongoing/not yet ended, or when the results of which are relevant today. It can be translated into English as the "I have done.." type of expression.

## Conjugations

This tense is formed by using haber in its *present* tense conjugation, in combination with the *past participle* of the target verb.

### *Form of Haber*

| Singular       | Ending | Plural           | Ending   |
|:---------------|-------:|:-----------------|---------:|
| Yo             | he     | Nosotros         | hemos    |
| Tu             | has    | Vosotros         | habéis   |
| Él/Ella/Usted  | ha     | Ellos/as/Ustedes | han      |

Note that the 3rd person conjugation 'ha' is used here, never 'hay.' Recall that 'hay' serves as both the singular AND plural impersonal expression (there is/are)--like the Italian "c'é" and "ci sono"--and is never used in these compound tenses.

The past participles of verbs are discussed [HERE], but can generally be formed by changing the ending of the infinitive form of a verb to '-ado' for '-ar' verbs annd '-ido' for '-er' and '-ir' verbs. There are some irregulars to be aware of, and one can refer to the file mentioned previously to find a list of these.
