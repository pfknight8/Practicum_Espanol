# Big 4 Irregulars

These four verbs are core to spanish

### Estar

- To be
- Similar to Ser, but Estar is used for more "temporary" qualities

| Verb Tense          | Yo        | Tu        | Él/Ella/Usted | Nosotros     | Vosotros    | Ellos/as/Ustedes |

| Preterite Ind.      | estuve    | estuviste | estuvo        | estuvimos    | estuvisteis | estuvieron       |
| Imperfect Ind.      | estaba    | estabas   | estaba        | estábamos    | estabais    | estaban          |

Present Participle: estando
Past Participle: estado

### Haber

- To have
- Distinct from Tener--which is used to indicate possesion--haber is used in the more abstract sense of 'having.'
- Haber is used as the auxiliary verb in perfect tenses--"I have done __" = "he hecho __."
- It is used to state "there is/are" something--like "c'é/ci sono" in Italian--in the form of "hay"
- It is used in the expression "hay que" to state "it is necessary (that)" such as "¡Hay que prestar atención!"

|:--------------------|:-------:|:--------:|:-------------:|:----------:|:---------:|:----------------:|
| Present Indicative  | he      | has      | ha            | hemos      | habéis    | han              |
| Preterite Ind.      | hube    | hubiste  | hubo          | hubimos    | hubisteis | hubieron         |
| Imperfect Ind.      | había   | habías   | había         | habíamos   | habíais   | habían           |
| Future Ind.         | habré   | habrás   | habrá         | habremos   | habréis   | habrán           |
| Conditional         | habría  | habrías  | habría        | habríamos  | habríais  | habrían          |
| Present Subjunctive | haya    | hayas    | haya          | hayamos    | hayáis    | hayan            |
| Imperfect Subj.     | hubiera | hubieras | hubiera       | hubiéramos | hubierais | hubieran         |
| Preterite Subj.     | hubiese | hubieses | hubiese       | hubiésemos | hubieseis | hubiesen         |
| Future Subj.        | hubiere | hubieres | hubiere       | hubiéremos | hubiereis | hubieren         |

Present Participle: habiendo

### Tener

- To have
- Distinct from 'Haber,' Tener is used to indicate actual possesion or holding something.
- Unlike in Italian, which uses this ONLY for actually physically holding something, here it can be the general possesion, such as "I have a house down the street."
- It is not used as an auxiliary verb.
- It is used in "tener que" expressions for 'having to do something,' such as in "Me tengo que ir ahora" = "I have to go now."
