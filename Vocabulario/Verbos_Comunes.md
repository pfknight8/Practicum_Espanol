# Common Verbs

Vocabulary list of verbs

## ¿?

### Hacer

- To do; to make

### Decir

- To say; to tell

### Poder

- To be able to

### Poner

- To put; to place

### Ir

- To go

### Saber

- To know (facts/skills)
  - No sé cómo cocinar eso. = I do not know how to cook that.
  - ¿Quien sabe? = Who knows?
  - Él sabe la respuesta. = He knows the answer.

### Conocer

- To know (familiarity)
  - Conozco este restaurante. = I know (am familiar with) this restaurant.
  - ¿Conoces a mi esposa? = Do you know my wife?

### Querer

- To want

### Necesitar

- To need

### Venir

- To come

### Llegar

- To arrive

### Llevar

- To carry

### Saltir

- To jump

### Correr

- To run

### Caminar

- To walk (at/to some place)
  - Most direct, neutral, and practical meaning of walk; implies a purpose, destination, being done for need/strenuous exercise.
  - The word for path--camino--is derived from this, useful for remembering to use caminar when a place/destination is in mind

### Pasear

- To walk (stroll)
  - More leisurely, like going for a stroll, taking a tour around town, wandering aimlessly, walking a pet, etc.
  - More in line with the Italian passegiare, emphasis is on the enjoyment of the activity of walking in itself.

### Andar

- To walk (act of); to go (around); to move about
  - More versatile, with a broder meaning than 'ir' or 'caminar,' as a general expression of movement.
  - Can be used to describe the action of walking.
  - Usage varies colloquially, so it may depend on where in the Spanish speaking world you are.
  - For example, "¡Ándale!" = "Go!" or "Hurry up!"
  - Some places may use it in place of 'estar,' both alone and in progressive tenses ("going"), such as "Ando en el centro." = "I am going/am downtown."

### Bajar(se)

- To go down; to lower; (ref) to get off/out of
  - Bajar is the more common word to use for going hown stairs, or walking down a hill, etc.
  - It may also be used for lowering, even in abstract cases like "lowering one's standards."
  - Reflexive form is commonly used to describe disembarking a vehicle. "Me bajo del autobús." = "I'm getting off the bus."

### Descender

- To descend; to go down
  - Like bajar, but used in more formal contexts.
  - To physically go down, or decrease in value/rank.
  - May be used for specialized/official actions, like "descending a mountain" or going down in an elevator.
  - Literary, official, and scientific/economic situations will use this over bajar.

### Parar

- To stop; to halt movement
  - A more temporary interruption, often used for everday movement (vehicles, people, machinery, etc)
  - Can be used to describe taking a break (or needing to) "Necesito parar un momento" = "I need to break for a moment"
  - In Latin America, it may be used as "to stand up" or "to set something upright."

### Detener(se)

- To stop
  - A more deliberate, forceful, or intentional stop.
  - Can be used like "detain," as well as delaying or impeding.
  - Reflexive form (Detenerse) means to pause, or halt an action.
  - Can often be used interchangeably with Parar when talking about physically stopping something/one.

### Dejar

- To leave; to quit
  - Worth mentioning with "stop" words: "dejar de" often translates to stop in English, but this is only in the case of quitting a habit or ongoing aciton: "dejar de fumar" = "stop smoking." In these cases, the word is generally replacable with 'quit,' which is what 'dejar' actually means.
  - To leave, in this case, means to leave something behind, whether abandoned, forgotten, or placed.
  - Can be used for physically leaving a place, but will indicate abandonment/leaving forever.
  - Can be used to express giving permission "Te dejaré __." = "I will let you __."
  - Also used for quitting, and is less formal (and more common in speech) than Renunciar

### Renunciar

- To renounce; to quit/resign
  - More formal than dejar, would be used in a formal resignation, or ceding of rights/privileges in an official capacity.

### Salir

- To leave; to go out
  - Used for the act of departing from some place.
  - Can be used for going out socially, kind of like 'uscire' in Italian.
