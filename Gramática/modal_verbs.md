# Modal Verbs

Modal verbs are verbs that are able to be followed by an infinitive. They can be simple, such as using "quiero bailar" for "I want to dance," or have additional rules/words to insert, such as "tengo *que* trabajar" for "I have to work."

## Simple modals

- Deber = to must/should
- Querer = to want to
- Poder = to be able to (can)
- Saber = to know how to
- Soler = to usually do

## Modals that take 'que'

- Tener que = to have to
- Pensar que = to think that

## Odd cases

- Hay que = one must (impersonal)

This is the way to impersonalize commands/things that a general someone ought to do.

Examples:
"Hay que limpiar el piso" = "One must clean the floor"
"¡Hay que trabajar con esfuerzo!" = "We have to work hard!"

## Other Info

In english, one might be familiar with these as the so called "helping verbs" such as 'could, should, would' as well as words like can, may, might, shall, ought to, etc. These are verbs that are used to qualify or condition another. In English, these words take these singular forms, existing in their own right without relation to who is speaking or what tense the surrounding words may be in.

In spanish, however, these modal verbs are what actually takes the conjugation, both in terms of person speaking and tense. In many cases, this conjugation is actually what dictates to which English words they translate to.

For example,

"Debe ir a trabajar" = "He *must* go to work"
"Debería ir a trabajar" = "He *should* go to work"

Where the first is 'deber' in the present tense, and the second is that same verb in the conditional (present) tense.
